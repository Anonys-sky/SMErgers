# Event-team survey (31 Oct 2026)

A short Google Form sent by the team to event professionals in its own network. This page holds only the **aggregated** answers. The raw sheet contains respondents' names and companies, so it is not in the repository.

## Read this first: what the survey is and is not

- **7 responses from 5 companies.** Three of the seven responses came from one company, so the real number of companies is five.
- **All are event businesses** (dinners, launches, weddings, AV, hotel events). Nothing here tells us about renovation, agencies or print.
- **A convenience sample** from the team's own network, not a random sample. It is a first signal, not a statistic about Malaysian SMEs.
- **Self-reported estimates.** The percentages and ringgit figures are respondents' own guesses.
- **Question 13 described the product favourably**, and **question 14 offered our own price tiers**. So the usefulness score is not neutral, and the price answers show which of our tiers people picked, not what they would pay unprompted.
- The usefulness question is read as a 1 to 5 scale (5 = most useful). **Confirm the scale labels in the form.**

## The problem

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| Where do project updates and scope changes happen? | WhatsApp: **7 of 7**. Phone or in person: 4. Email: 2. Project software: 2. |
| How often do clients request extras casually in WhatsApp after signing? | Almost every project (80 to 100%): **3**. Most projects (50 to 79%): **2**. Some projects (20 to 49%): 2. |
| Most frequent additions | Headcount or quantity: **6 of 7**. Overtime: 5. Equipment upgrades: 5. Urgent add-ons with no price stated: 4. |
| How do you find the changes at billing time? | Scrolling back through weeks of chat from memory: **4 of 7**. No consistent system: 1. Finance checks the chat or charges first and refunds later: 2. |
| How often are agreed extras left off the bill or written off? | Frequently: 2. Occasionally: 3. Rarely: 1. One answered in words, not in a category. |
| Share of quote value lost on a typical job with changes | More than 15%: **3**. 11 to 15%: 2. 6 to 10%: 2. Using the **bottom of each range**, the average answer is **about 11%**. |
| Revenue lost per year | More than RM40,000: 3. RM15,001 to 40,000: 1. RM5,001 to 15,000: 2. RM2,000 to 5,000: 1. |
| Hours a month spent reconciling chats and quotes | 11 hours or more: **6 of 7** (two said more than 20, one wrote "more than the stated"). 5 to 10 hours: 1. |

## The reaction to the solution

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| How useful would a tool that reads the group, links each change to its message and needs PM, director and client confirmation be? (1 to 5) | **5: three people. 4: three people. 3: one person.** Average **4.3**. Six of seven rated it 4 or 5. |
| Fair monthly price if it recovered extras and halved billing time | **RM250 a month tier: 4.** RM100 a month tier: 2. "No idea, I need to ask my boss": 1. |
| One owner wrote | An invitation to meet the team and talk (a possible pilot conversation). |

## Free-text answers worth quoting (shortened, anonymised; ask before showing a name)

- Owner, event company, 100+ jobs a year: "Clueless employee adds this and that, then their boss doesn't admit and doesn't want to pay."
- Hotel event operations manager: "Most clients don't know that adding rehearsal time costs money, around RM200 to RM500. They keep adding time even though we told them. In the end they don't want to admit it."
- Wedding venue business that partners with caterers and decorators: "It often causes friction when too much stuff is lost in chats."
- Finance staff, event company: "Both the client and our team give me headaches when both sides ask to add things at the last minute."

## What this supports, and what it does not

**Supports:** the problem is real in event companies: WhatsApp is where changes happen for all seven, most jobs are affected for five of seven, and about 11% of quote value is the lower-bound average estimate of what is lost. Our worked example (11.2% of one simulated quote) is in the same range. The RM100 and RM250 tiers were both chosen by people.

**Does not support:** accuracy of our reader; real savings (the estimates are guesses); demand outside event companies; a willingness to pay without our prompt; anything about customers who are not in the team's network.

**Next step:** talk to the owner who asked to meet, and run the pilot with three to five firms to measure the real unbilled rate.
